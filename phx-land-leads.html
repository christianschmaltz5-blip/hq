🏠 Custom Home Calculator — Build It Yourself

For when you buy the land and build the homes. Pick a lead (or type your own numbers) and it tells you the sale price each home needs to hit, and the most you can pay for the land. The cost numbers start as placeholders — replace them with real quotes.
How it works: per home, cost = build + design/permit fees + site work + its share of the land, plus loan interest. Each home must sell for enough to cover that cost, the selling costs, and your profit. "Most you can pay" works that backward from your expected sale price. Build cost, site work and sale price are the three numbers that matter most — get real local quotes and recent custom-home sales before trusting the answer. Not an appraisal; check zoning, lot-split rules, water/sewer and access first.

💰 Max Offer — Buy the Lot, Sell It to a Builder

Use this if you plan to flip the land. Max offer = the most you can pay the owner and still keep your profit. Open lower than that. (Planning to build the homes yourself? Use the Custom Home Calculator below.)
More settings (closing costs, lot splits, sorting)
How it works: nearby new-build homes sold for about the "home value" shown. A builder pays roughly 25% of that for the lot (a rule of thumb — confirm with a real builder, it moves every number). Max offer = that lot price minus your profit and closing costs (and demolition, if the lot has an old building). Home values are medians of recent County Assessor sales, not appraisals. Lot splits need city approval.

Call Scripts

Plain-spoken scripts for calling an owner. Swap in the [brackets] before you dial.

🏛️ Arizona LLC / Business Search Find an LLC's managers, members and filings. Enter the owner name from the lead card in Business Name and choose Contains.

Phone call — opening and questions 
OPENING
"Hey, is this [Name]? This is Christian. I'm a local real estate developer. I'm calling about the vacant land on [street] near [cross street]. Is that one yours?"
(If they ask how you got the number: "It's the owner on the county records.")

"I'll be quick. I'm looking at that land to build custom homes with a builder partner, and I heard you might be open to selling. Did I catch you at an okay time?"

QUESTIONS (don't name a price first, let them talk)
- Are you thinking about selling it, or only at the right price?
- How long have you held it? What was your plan for it?
- Is it just you on the title, or are there other partners I'd need to talk to?
- Have you had it listed, or had other offers?
- What would you need to see to feel good about selling?
- Do you know if water and sewer reach the lot, and how you get in from the street?

IF THEY GIVE A PRICE
"Okay, thank you, that helps. Let me run it with my partner and see if the numbers work. Can I call you back by [day]?"
(Don't agree on the spot. Check it against the Custom Home Calculator's max land price first.)
Phone call — objections and closing 
IF THEY HESITATE
- "I'm not selling." -> "No problem at all. Can I check back in a few months, in case things change?"
- "What's your offer?" -> "I'd rather understand the land before I throw a number out. What would make you say yes?"
- "Are you a realtor?" -> "No, I'm a developer. I buy land and build homes. I'm not trying to list it."
- "How did you get my number?" -> "From the county ownership records."

CLOSING
"Thanks for talking with me, [Name]. If it's okay, I'll send you a quick email so you have my name and number. And if you think of anything about the lot, call me anytime."
(Get their best email and phone. Thank them either way.)
Voicemail (about 20 seconds) 
Hi [Name], this is Christian. I'm a local developer, and I'm calling about your vacant land near [street / cross street]. I'd love to talk for a couple of minutes if you're ever open to selling. You can call me at [number]. Thanks.
Partnership pitch — "develop it with you" 
OPENING
"Hi [Name], this is Christian. I'm a local builder and a licensed Realtor, and I'm looking at your lot on [street]. Before anything else, I'm not calling to lowball you or push you to sell. Do you have two minutes?"

THE PITCH
"I build custom homes on lots like yours, and what I'd like to explore is doing it WITH you. Instead of buying your land for a flat price, you keep an ownership share in the project. When the homes sell, you take your share of the profit, which can be more than a straight sale would pay."

"I do the work: design, permits, construction, and the sale. You don't manage anything. And I'd check zoning and any HOA rules first, at my cost, so we only move forward if it really works."

"Would it be worth a short conversation, or a coffee, to see if the numbers make sense for you?"

(Disclose your license and your interest in the deal. Don't promise returns. Share the assumptions, not guarantees.)
Partnership — structures and objections 
THREE WAYS TO STRUCTURE IT (simplest first)
1. Option, then partner: they give you 6-12 months to get zoning, lot-split and HOA answers before anything closes. Pay a small real option fee ($1,000-$5,000).
2. Land-for-equity joint venture: they contribute the land at an agreed value; you bring plans, permits, build and sale; profit is split by a written formula (for example, their land value back first, then a split of the rest).
3. Hybrid: some cash up front, plus a share of the profit after the build.

IF THEY PUSH BACK
- "How do I know you'll pay me?" -> "Fair question. It's all in a written agreement your attorney reviews, and I'll show you the numbers. You don't have to trust me, you can check."
- "I just want cash." -> "That's fine, and I'd give you a cash price too. I only ask that you hear the partnership numbers first."
- "What if it loses money?" -> "It can. I'll show you the downside case before you decide. Nothing is guaranteed."
- "I bought it recently." -> "Then you know what it's worth to you. A partnership lets you keep the upside instead of stopping at today's price."

BEFORE YOU SIGN ANYTHING
- Have an attorney write the agreement. Profit-sharing with outside owners can involve partnership and securities rules.
- Base the agreed land value on comps, not on what the owner hopes.
Before you dial 
- Don't say you "have the money" or that a lender is approved unless that's true.
- Use the cash scripts if they want to sell; use the partnership pitch if you want to develop with them. Don't bring up your own partner split (the 50/50) unless they ask.
- Check the Do Not Call registry before calling any individual's personal number. Calls to a business line are fine.
- Take notes during the call (their price, timing, who owns what) and put them in the lead's notes box.
