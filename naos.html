Scottsdale NAOS Guide

NAOS (natural area open space) is the share of an ESL lot that must stay natural: no house, pool or grading there.

NAOS table

% of lot that must stay natural

Worked example, 1 acre: Lower Desert at 0-2% slope = 20% natural, 0.2 ac natural / 0.8 ac usable. Upper Desert at 10-15% = 45%, 0.45 / 0.55. Hillside over 25% = 80%, 0.8 / 0.2. The last row of the table is the minimum after allowed reductions; underwrite on the box number, not the minimum.

How to read it

  1. Find the landform column from the city ESL map (Lower Desert, Upper Desert or Hillside).
  2. Find the slope row from the topo.
  3. Read the box: that is the share that must stay natural (no house, pool or grading).
  4. Usable acres = the rest (lot acres x (1 - box)).
  5. Gray is not all buildable: setbacks, washes and HOA rules still bite.
  6. Underwrite on the box number, not the minimum.

Source

The table comes from the City of Scottsdale ESL Overlay page, scottsdaleaz.gov/codes/ESLO (primary), which points to Zoning Ordinance Sec. 6.1060 for reductions. The landform descriptions (lower desert under 5% slope, upper desert 5-35%, hillside 15% to over 50%) and the 134 sq mile coverage come from the older Citizen's Guide to ESL, so the ESL page and the ordinance take priority. Read Sec. 6.1060 or confirm with Scottsdale planning before relying on it.

Lead figures on this site are estimates: slope is averaged from USGS 3DEP elevation samples inside the parcel, landform comes from the city's ESLO Landforms map layer. The official NAOS uses the surveyed topo, and Sec. 6.1060 reductions are not modeled.
